The claim

Score-aware AI interpretation ships nowhere. Generators produce finished audio with no score and no interpretive layer; DAWs render scores mechanically; adaptive engines (Endel/LifeScore) keep the composer material proprietary and output functional music. The interpretation axis — a sanctioned, inspectable, licensed space between score and audio — is unclaimed.

Why text-to-audio can't occupy the axis

Generation answers "make me a track." Interpretation answers "perform this work, within these sanctions, and prove provenance." Text-to-audio has no fixed work to interpret, no sanctioned space to stay inside, and no manifest to disclose. The axis is defined by the artifact (score + prompt + rights), not by model quality — a better generator still isn't a conductor.

The legal environment

  • Post-settlement licensed future: the majors' lawsuits against Suno/Udio ended in licensed, opt-in generation deals (Udio+UMG "Starstruck", Suno+WMG). Generation is being pulled onto licensed rails — our schema-first, manifest-mandatory design was built for exactly this world.
  • Schema-first is a legal asset: a human-authored seed (the interpretive schema) is copyrightable — it strengthens provenance and authorship claims over the AI-performed output, and it is the thing a rights holder can actually license.

Risk register

  • Label walled gardens — Starstruck-style deals could fence the "many sanctioned renditions" marketplace before it opens. Mitigation: the open spec + independent corpus ladder; the artifact is portable by design.
  • Patent FTO — Endel/LifeScore/Reactional hold adaptive/generative-music patents; a freedom-to-operate review is a pre-build requirement for the engine, not an afterthought.
  • Niche composer-tool adoption — the authoring craft (seeds, budgets, assertions) is expert-facing; adoption risk concentrates in the workbench's usability, which is why the loop tooling came first.
